Using Veyl

Provide liquidity and swap on Solana without your wallet showing up.

Getting started

Open the app

Open the app and press Get started. You don't need a wallet: choose No wallet needed and Veyl makes a deposit address in your browser. Send SOL, USDC or USDT to it from any wallet or exchange, then move it into your private balance. Prefer your own wallet? Connect Phantom, Solflare or Backpack instead. Veyl lists 83 pools: SOL–USDC and the other majors, plus every major Meteora pair. Pools, positions and fees are read from the chain.

Every pool is curated. Veyl lists each pool itself, choosing the pair, price step and fee, so there are no copycat or spam pools to sort through. You can add liquidity to any of them; you can't open a new pool yet. Open pool creation is on the roadmap.

The deposit address

Its key lives only in this browser and goes into your encrypted backup. Keep about 0.01 SOL in it for network fees. Anything left over can be sent out from the account menu with Send what's left out; that transfer is public, so for privacy withdraw from your private balance instead.

Your Veyl wallet, and its seed

The app makes a private account on first open. Its seed lives only in this browser and is the only thing that can read or spend your private balance: your seed is your balance. Choose Back up (encrypted) in the account menu early; clear your browser data without a backup and the funds are gone.

Private · Public

Every action panel has this toggle. Private pays from your private balance: your browser makes a proof, a relayer submits it, your wallet never appears. Public pays from your wallet, signed by it, like any Solana app. On the Portfolio page, Deposit moves tokens into your private balance (public; admitted after a moment) and Withdraw pays a Veyl address (hidden) or a fresh Solana address (amount and recipient public).

What stays private

What stays hidden

  • Your private balance How much you hold, in which tokens.
  • Private sends Who paid whom, and how much.
  • Your positions Who owns them and how large they are.
  • Fees you claim They go straight into your private balance.
  • Sealed swap sizes Only the batch's total is decrypted.
  • Which deposit paid for what A proof shows you own funds, not which.

What's public

  • Deposits and withdrawals Wallet, token and amount in; amount and recipient out.
  • Everything at a pool Prices, liquidity per bin, each swap's amounts, each liquidity action's change per bin (never who).
  • Sealed batches as a whole Totals, seller counts, clearing price; never one person's size.
  • The token of a shielded action The relayer's fee is paid in it.
  • Timing When things happened, not who did them.

Keep yourself private

  1. Pay out to a fresh address. The app refuses your deposit wallet and any address with history.
  2. Wait between depositing and using. Acting right after a deposit links the two by timing.
  3. Avoid amounts that match a deposit. Withdrawing exactly what was just deposited gives it away.
  4. Prefer a Veyl address over a Solana address when sending out; a public payout names its recipient.
  5. Use a VPN or Tor. The relayer sees the network address a request comes from.

Two honest notes

Small crowds are weaker. Privacy comes from being one of many; a pool or batch with few users is easier to read. Treat privacy as thin until there is real volume.

The committee. Sealed swap totals are decrypted by a group of 3 servers. If 2 teamed up they could see individual sealed-swap sizes, never take funds: a wrong answer is rejected on chain, and if they go quiet the batch refunds.

Provide liquidity privately

Pick a pool

Open Pools, choose a pair, and keep Private selected in the Add panel.

Pick a shape

Spot spreads your tokens evenly across the range. Curve concentrates them around the current price. Bid-Ask weights the edges, for an expected move.

Pick a range and size

Type a minimum and maximum price or pick a preset width; only bins the price trades through earn fees. Enter one or both tokens. A shielded position covers up to 16 bins.

Confirm

Your browser makes a proof (a few seconds; longer on phones), the relayer submits it, the position appears in your Portfolio. The chain sees liquidity arrive in those bins, not who added it.

What "shielded" means here

All shielded positions sit under one shared on-chain owner; a private note says which part is yours. Nobody can see who owns a position or how large it is, and your later actions on it are not linked.

Fees accrue, then claim

Every swap through your bins pays you its fee. Press Claim on the position: the amount lands in your private balance, never shown on chain.

Rebalance

If the price moved, Rebalance re-places your liquidity around it in one action, keeping your shape.

Remove

Remove takes 25%, 50% or all of it back into your private balance as new notes, fees included; the chain sees shares leave, not whose.

Swap

Instant

Settles now at the current pool price. Paid from your shielded balance it hides who you are; the amounts in and out are public at the pool, as with any swap. Use it when you want the trade done now and the size is not sensitive.

Sealed

Hides the size too. Your amount is encrypted on your device and joins the pool's current batch (about a minute). Only the batch's totals are decrypted; buyers are netted against sellers, one swap covers the difference, and you Claim your share at the clearing price with a second proof. Use it when your size is sensitive; it settles with the batch and has no limit price, only a guard against the price drifting too far. A side with under three sellers waits for the next batch, and is refunded if it never fills.

Limit orders, Zap, Rewards

Limit orders

An order sits in one price bin next to the current price. Swaps that cross it fill it at that price; it earns that bin's fee share while it waits. Withdraw any time, filled or not.

Zap

Have only one of the pair's tokens? Zap swaps part of it in the pool and adds both sides at once.

Rewards

Some pools pay farm rewards to liquidity in the active bin. For now shielded positions do not earn farm rewards, only swap fees; a public position earns both.

Roadmap

  • Now · Private liquidity, instant and sealed swaps, limit orders, zap and rewards on Solana.
  • Next · Public key ceremony, where independent people each add to the keys behind the proofs, followed by an independent audit.
  • Later · Open pool creation, so any project can list its own pair, with a listing bond and checks. Rewards for shielded positions, limit prices on sealed swaps, larger private positions, Jupiter routing, viewing keys for accountants, hardware-wallet support.

FAQ

Can I lose funds?

Yes, as with any liquidity protocol: concentrated liquidity can lose value against simply holding when the price leaves your range. Fees offset that; they do not guarantee it. Start small while you learn the app.

What if I lose my seed?

Your private balance and positions are gone; nobody can recover them, including us. Back up from the account menu: the file is encrypted with your passphrase, and restoring it in another browser brings everything back.

Why is proving slow on my phone?

Every shielded action is a proof your device makes itself, and a phone has far less power than a laptop: expect two to four times longer, sealed swaps most of all. The first proof also downloads a key of a few megabytes.

Why can't I pay out to the wallet I deposited from?

Anyone watching could line up the deposit and the payout, which undoes the point. The app refuses that wallet and any address with a history.

Is this audited?

Veyl has had two internal security reviews, with every finding fixed. An independent audit and the public key ceremony are the next items on the roadmap.

Can I create my own pool?

Not yet. Every pool is listed by Veyl, which keeps out copycat and scam pairs and keeps liquidity deep where it counts. Open pool creation, with a listing bond and duplicate checks, is on the roadmap.

What does the relayer see?

Your request and the network address it came from. It cannot redirect funds, raise its fee or change what you asked for: all of that is locked inside your proof. It never sees your balance or who you are.

Is Veyl a mixer? Who screens deposits?

No. A publisher checks each deposit against a deny list before admitting it, keeping stolen or sanctioned funds out; until then you can take it back, and the admitted list is public. The market stays fully visible: Veyl hides who you are and how much you hold, not what the market did.
