# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Crypto users who are new to providing liquidity. They have a Solana wallet and have swapped tokens before, but have never
set a price range, picked a fee tier or used a privacy pool. They arrive wanting to earn fees on tokens they already hold,
without the whole chain seeing their positions. They know what a wallet, a token and a transaction are; they don't know
bins, shapes, volatility fees, notes, relayers or sealed batches. Experienced LPs (Meteora, Orca) are a second audience
and must still find every pro tool, one step further in.

## Product Purpose
Veyl is private concentrated liquidity on Solana: DLMM-style bin pools where nobody can link a position, swap or fee back
to the wallet. Liquidity is the product, privacy is the edge, swapping is a side feature. Success is a newcomer who opens
a private position, understands what they hold and what it earns, and can get their money out, without reading the docs
and without a step that makes them doubt where their money is.

## Brand Personality
Calm, exact, discreet. It talks like a careful person explaining a bank transfer: short sentences, concrete numbers,
plain words (Private / Public, Deposit / Withdraw). It never hypes and never claims safety it hasn't earned (no audit
badges, no fake stats; the "not yet independently audited" line stays). Positioning line: "Concentrated liquidity, kept
private." Reference feel: Meteora and Delta Liquidity for the LP tools (take inspiration, never copy), Apple for restraint.

## Anti-references
- Generic DeFi dashboards: neon gradients, glowing cards, rows of hero metrics, "APY 4,000%" bait.
- Anything that looks AI-generated or template-built: grid backgrounds, eyebrow kickers on every section, identical card
  grids, serif display fonts.
- Fake trust: testimonials, partner logos, audit or security badges, invented volume.
- Jargon walls: an LP panel that shows every parameter at once with no explanation.

## Design Principles
1. One clear path, everything still reachable. The default view shows the next sensible step; advanced options sit one
   click away ("More options"), never removed.
2. Say what happens to the money. Every action names what is public, what is private, what it costs and where the funds
   end up, in plain words, before the user confirms.
3. Explain at the point of doubt. A term is explained where it appears (tooltip, hint line), not in a glossary page.
4. No dead ends. Every error says what to do next and offers the button to do it; every empty state points to the
   first action.
5. Numbers you can trust. Real figures or a dash, units always shown, never a guess dressed as a fact.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.1 AA: 4.5:1 contrast for body and muted text (3:1 for large text), full keyboard use with visible focus, labels for
screen readers on every control and icon button, and every animation respecting prefers-reduced-motion. Don't use color
alone for meaning (Private/Public, in range/out of range also carry text or an icon).
